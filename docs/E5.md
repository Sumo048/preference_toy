Session E5, branch `art/confirm-E5` (also pushed to `claude/confirm-e1-gated-graft-xohqq9`), from `origin/art/explore-E1`.

- The graft beats even the oracle-selected B1b in 65 of 70 cells.
- **B6 is far better than the graft in every cell** (D 0.017–0.035 vs 0.067–0.121). B6 retrains all weights on
  track-masked π_ref samples, which removes 99.4–99.9% of the forgotten mass (Removed 0.994–0.999). The graft adds gated units beside a
  frozen π_ref, and it leaves 8–18% of the forgotten mass (Removed 0.82–0.97). Its residual is the redirect, not
  detection (E1's `diag_resid.py`). Both methods use the same label-free ingredient: a behaviour tracker seeded by the
  logged negatives. The difference lies in how each edits the policy.

Per-cell D for all four arms is in `explore/E5/results/tables.md`.

## What was run

- **Method**: K10c exactly as `explore/E1/graft.py` runs it (config in the prereg). E1's `common.py` and `graft.py` are
  imported unchanged. `explore/E5/e5.py` adds only settings and seeds 5–9 to `load_cell`, plus output paths. It also splits
  the unit-fit loop into resumable 250-iteration chunks, with the checkpoint holding graft params, Adam state,
  batch-generator state and iteration.
- **Checks before the prereg** (dev seed 0): (1) chunked == unchunked, bit for bit (`test_chunk.py`); (2) the full K10c
  fit of k4 s0 n4 reproduces E1's stored graft bit for bit in both rounds, with D 0.0875 = E1's 0.0875 and all metrics
  equal (`repro_dev.py`, `logs/repro_dev.log`; round 2 finished after the prereg push, as the prereg says).
- **Grid**: 7 settings × seeds 5–9. One npm-4 fit per (setting, seed), 35 fits in total. That graft was then evaluated
  on the npm-4 and npm-16 cells, 70 cells in total.
- **npm 16**: in 34 of 35 (setting, seed) pairs, the npm-16 cell's tracker names the same bad tracks as the npm-4 cell.
  The npm-4 graft is reused there, so its D is identical at both npm. **alt s7 npm 16** named one extra bad track (275,
  a small track that one of the 16 negatives falls into). Per the prereg rule, it got its own K10c fit: D 0.115 vs 0.117
  for the npm-4 graft. The npm-16 B1a pairs therefore differ from the npm-4 pairs only through B1a, except in that cell.
- **Tracker**: bad-flag precision ≥ 0.9999 in all 70 cells (oracle used for scoring only). Recall is 0.82–0.88, the
  same as E1. Bad tracks per cell: 4 (n_forget 4), 2 (k2), 1 (k1), plus the alt s7 n16 case above.
- **Rounds**: round 2 lowers D from 0.105 to 0.099 on average over the 35 npm-4 fits.
- **Baselines** were not rerun. B0, B1a and B1b come from `origin/art/arms` `results/baselines/summary.json`
  (`scripts/cond_cell.py`; snapshot in `explore/E5/baselines/arms_summary.json`). B6 comes from E4's results
  (snapshot in `explore/E5/baselines/e4_rows.json`).
- **Compute**: 4 workers × 1 thread. Every checkpointed unit stopped at ≤ 806 s and final units were shorter, so every
  unit stayed under 20 min. One fit takes about 22 min wall over 2 units, and the sum of `fit_s` over the 35 npm-4 fits is
  4.9 CPU-hours. The full grid ran from about 17:55 to 21:21 UTC with 0 failed units.
- **Rendering**: `explore/E5/render_check.py` loads the committed `.pt` files with E1's standalone loader
  (`explore/E1/weights/load.py`, `GraftedPolicy`). It reproduces the recorded D exactly (checked on k4_s5_n4,
  alt_s7_n16, k1_s5_n4 and b3p1_s9_n16). Each file is a list of 2 per-round tensor dicts.

## Deviations from the prereg

1. `explore/E5/run_queue.py` was changed after the prereg push. The change makes it restart-safe: a unit already running
   is not started again, and the worker count is re-read from `logs/P`. This let the run go from 3 to 4 workers after the
   dev repro finished. Scheduling only; its sha256 is now `8a480f8e…` (prereg: `ec038bcf…`). `e5.py` and `analyze.py`
   are unchanged (sha256 as in the prereg).
2. No other deviation. No cell was rerun or excluded, and every cell has both arms (N = 10 per setting).

## Caveats

- K10c's fit fix (lr 1e-3, clip 1) was chosen on E1's dev seed 4. On fresh seeds no fit diverged: the worst cell is
  k4 s7 at 0.153, and every cell beats B1a.
- The npm-4 and npm-16 cells share one graft, so the paired N = 10 overstates independence. The seed-clustered
  numbers above give the same verdicts.
- Like E1, the fit needs about 1.7 M π_ref decodes plus backprop per cell, far above a gate budget. It also adds 23%
  parameters (19,072 trainable per round, 38,144 over 2 rounds, in every setting).
- B6 dominates the graft on D in every cell. The graft's selling point is that π_ref stays untouched and the edit is
  locally gated, not that it gets the lowest D.
