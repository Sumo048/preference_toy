Self-Paired DPO Study

We edit a robot policy with negative labels only. This study tests self-paired DPO and its variants on a long-course robot toy. The best variant raises clean success from 79.1% to 93.2%. It cuts the negative rate from 18.5% to 0.4–4.1%.

Held-out courses, mean of 5 seeds. "From" is the frozen base policy on the same courses.

Terms

The test bed

A point robot drives a course of 4 waypoint segments. Each segment has 1 to 3 disc obstacles.

A contact does not stop the episode. The robot slides along the obstacle and continues.

The policy is a chunked rectified flow. It decodes 8 steps of velocity from noise, then replans. An episode has at most 18 chunks.

An episode is negative when it touches an obstacle or when its jerk goes above a threshold. A run that does not reach the goal in time is not negative.

The frozen base policy reaches the goal in 94.5% of episodes. Its clean success is 79.1%. Its negative rate is 18.5%.

Each labelled course gives 1 to 3 negative episodes. The data has up to 512 labelled courses. All numbers below come from 128 held-out courses.

Finding 1

The training dose controls the result

We train each method for k steps per labelled row. We sweep k from 1/32 to 16.

Pinned SDPO is the strongest method at a small dose. It gains +12.0 points with approach windows and +10.3 points with trajectory labels.

Pinned SDPO collapses at a large dose. Earlier runs at 5,000 steps showed this collapse only.

Fresh SDPO peaks lower, at +5.8 and +3.9 points. It stays positive over a wide range of k.

The best dose for pinned SDPO is close to 200 total steps at every dataset size. A rule that scales steps with the number of rows gives too many steps for large datasets.

NPO and KTO lose clean success at every dose. We do not use them further.

G (change in clean success, points) against k, one line per number of labelled courses Ns. Bars show ±2 standard errors over 5 seeds. Hover a point for its value.
Finding 2

A new reference without new negatives makes the policy worse

We test three variants that change πref or the winner noise. All use the same logged negatives.

Iterated SDPO starts a new SDPO run from the result of the last run. Each extra round lowers G. Pinned SDPO with trajectory labels at 512 courses goes from +9.2 to −8.0 to −51.0.

Iterated SDPO is also worse than one longer run. At 982 total steps, one run gives +6.7 and two rounds give −8.0.

A shifting πref inside one run also lowers G. Four or eight segments make the policy collapse.

Own-noise pinned SDPO scores each winner at its own noise. It peaks near +5 points, close to fresh SDPO. Averaging 8 winners does not help.

So the shared noise between winner and loser makes pinned SDPO strong.

Finding 3

New negatives at each segment help, up to a limit

A segment ends with two actions. We set πref to the current policy. We collect and label new negatives from the current policy on the same courses.

We keep the steps per segment fixed and increase the number of segments S from 1 to 64.

Fresh SDPO with trajectory labels gains the most from segments. At 512 courses it goes from +3.9 with one segment to +13.4 with 8 to 16 segments.

Pinned SDPO peaks after 1 to 4 segments. Pinned SDPO with approach windows reaches +14.1 at 512 courses with 2 segments.

Each configuration has a best S. After that point every configuration collapses.

Each segment needs a new round of labels. Eight segments use about eight times the labels of one run.

G against the number of segments S, one line per kseg (steps per segment per labelled row). S = 1 is a plain run.
Finding 4

After the limit, the two methods fail in different ways

Fresh SDPO stops the labelled failures and then stops finishing. At 64 segments its negative rate is 0.1%, but it reaches the goal in only 11% of episodes. It moves at 0.69 of the base speed.

The labels do not mark a timeout as negative. Fresh SDPO uses this gap.

Pinned SDPO becomes jerky. At 32 segments jerk occurs in 35–53% of episodes.

The negative rate keeps falling past the best point. Do not use it to choose when to stop.

Absolute rates on held-out courses at the best number of segments and past it. The base policy: goal reached 94.5%, clean success 79.1%, negative rate 18.5%.
Finding 5

Pinned SDPO moves the policy farther from the base

We retrain both methods at fixed step counts from 50 to 5,000 and measure four distances from the base.

At matched steps, pinned SDPO has 3–10 times the energy distance of fresh SDPO. Its KL surrogate is 5–200 times larger and grows with steps.

Its weight distance is 1.5–2.3 times larger. Its pathwise change is 1.5–4 times larger.

The energy distance compares sets of actions at a state. So the drift is a change of the action distribution, not only a new map from noise to action.

The drift is the same on negative states, labelled courses, unlabelled courses and held-out courses. It is not local to the labelled states.

At its best dose, pinned SDPO also gains more per unit of drift than fresh SDPO.

Drift from the base policy against training steps. Solid lines are the full losses. Dashed lines use only the winner term. Dotted lines use only the loser term. Log scales.
Finding 6

The loser term moves the policy and the winner term holds it back

We train each half of the loss alone.

The loser term alone destroys the policy for both methods. After 50 steps G is −40 to −67 points. Its energy distance is more than 10 times that of full pinned SDPO.

The winner term alone almost does not move the policy. Its gain is +1.6 to +3.1 points for pinned SDPO and close to 0 for fresh SDPO.

The gate of pinned SDPO closes to about 0.2 during training. The gate of fresh SDPO stays near 0.5.

Pinned SDPO scores winner and loser at the same noise. This gives a low-variance margin that the optimiser can satisfy. The optimiser satisfies it by moving the action distribution everywhere.

Fresh SDPO scores them at independent noises. The margin is noisy, the gate stays open, and the policy moves little.

Mean gate σ(β(rl − rw)) on a fixed probe batch during 5,000-step runs. A low gate means the margin is satisfied.

What to do next

Add an anchor term that holds the policy near the base on unlabelled states. Test whether pinned SDPO keeps its gain with less drift.

Stop each run at a fixed energy distance on unlabelled states. This rule needs no robot evaluation. Compare the methods at equal drift.

Label a timeout as negative. Test whether fresh SDPO then keeps improving with more segments.

The best doses on this page come from the same evaluation courses. A real robot run must choose its dose without that evaluation, so these peaks are optimistic.

Method notes

Every result uses 5 seeds. Each seed has its own base policy, courses and labelled negatives.

Each run is paired with a base-policy rollout in the same task, with the same noise. Rollouts on different machines differ in the last bits, so a pair from another task is not valid.

Training uses batch 256 and learning rate 10−4 with cosine decay. There is no KL term and no other regulariser.
