Long-Course Robot Toy

A point robot drives a chunked flow policy through four-segment obstacle courses. It usually succeeds, but sometimes bumps an obstacle or moves jerkily. Each bad run is labelled negative as a whole trajectory. This page shows what the robot does, what a negative looks like, and what each editing method does to the success and negative rates.

Starting point: the frozen base policy

Measured on 128 held-out courses × 32 rollouts per seed, 5 seeds. A negative is an episode with any obstacle contact or any jerk window above threshold. Clean success means reaching the last waypoint with neither.

The setup

Robot and course

  • A point agent in 2-D. A course has 4 waypoint segments, each with 1–3 disc obstacles. Segments turn by up to ±60°.
  • Touching an obstacle does not end the episode: the agent slides along the surface and keeps going.
  • Success = reaching the last waypoint within 144 steps.

Policy (π0-style)

  • A conditional rectified flow over action chunks: 8 steps of 2-D velocity, decoded from noise in 20 Euler steps, run open loop, then replanned. At most 18 chunks per episode.
  • Observation: position, offset to the current and next waypoint, the 3 nearest obstacles, last velocity.

Where failures come from

  • The base imitates demos (2,640 courses × 8). Demos contain rare injected bad chunks: heading into an obstacle, carrying momentum through a turn, and zig-zag jerk.
  • Most contacts are the policy's own drift into a bad state, not a single bad chunk.

The negative preference

  • Label: a whole episode is negative if it touches an obstacle or exceeds the jerk threshold anywhere.
  • Data: 1–3 negative episodes per labelled course, up to 512 courses (about 1,000 episodes, 15,000 chunks). Only about 7.5% of those chunks contain the event.
  • Trajectory labels (the real method): every chunk of the episode is a negative row. Window labels (oracle): only the event chunk and the 2 chunks before it.

The same episodes after editing

Each method edits the base policy, then replays the same noise sequence on the same course, so a difference comes only from the edit. Pick an episode. The grey path is the base and the coloured path is the edited policy.

The methods

Shared notation

ℓ(a; ξ, t, o) is the flow-matching loss of chunk a at noise ξ, time t and observation o. The log-ratio is r = ℓref − ℓθ: it is positive when the edited policy makes a chunk more likely than the base does.

Pinned SDPO (ours)

Self-paired DPO. The loser is the logged negative chunk at its logged noise. The winner is a fresh base sample at the same observation, scored at the same logged noise.

L = −log σ(β (r(a_w; ξ_log) − r(a_l; ξ_log))), β = 25

Fresh SDPO (ours)

The same pair, with winner and loser each scored at independent fresh noise. It changes the policy more gently, peaks lower and is harder to overtrain.

L = −log σ(β (r(a_w; ξ₁) − r(a_l; ξ₂))), β = 25

NPO (baseline)

Pushes down the likelihood of every logged negative chunk, with no positive term.

L = (2/β) softplus(β r(a_l; ξ)), β = 100

KTO (baseline)

Undesirable-only KTO. The reference point z₀ comes from mismatched rows.

L = 1 − σ(β (z₀ − r(a_l; ξ))), β = 100
